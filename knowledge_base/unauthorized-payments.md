# Unauthorized payments and refunds

## What the law says about refunds
When a payment was not authorized by the customer, the bank must, after a first check for fraud by the customer, refund the amount immediately and restore the account to the state it would have been in without the payment.
Source: Code de droit economique, Livre VII, payment services, text published by SPF Economie (economie.fgov.be/fr/legislation/livre-vii-code-de-droit), read on 2026-11-11.
The customer must report the payment without delay, and at the latest 13 months after the debit.
Source: Ombudsfin annual report 2022 (ombudsfin.be), read on 2026-11-11.

## When the bank can refuse
The bank can try to refuse a refund if it proves gross negligence by the customer, for example a PIN written on the card, or a card theft reported only the next day. The bank has to bring the proof. Use of the right code is not enough proof.
If the customer took part in the fraud, there is no protection.
Source: SPF Economie, page updated 2026-04-03 (economie.fgov.be/fr/themes/protection-des-consommateurs/stop-arnaques/victime-que-faire/vous-constatez-un-paiement-que/quand-la-banque-peut-elle), read on 2026-11-11.

## Phishing
In a phishing case the bank cannot invoke gross negligence if the customer could not reasonably suspect the fraud. The bank can only refuse if it proves the customer knew it was a scam or should have known.
Same source as above.

## What a reply may say
Explain the rules above in plain words. Ask for the date, the amount and the name on the statement. Say a colleague will review the case. Never say the refund is accepted or refused.

## Problems and what happens next
Every message about an unauthorized payment goes to a colleague, see the escalation rules.
